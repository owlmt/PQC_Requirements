# European Union Essential Services and Critical Assets Requirements for Post-Quantum Cryptography Migration

## TL;DR
- The EU regulates **entities, not assets**: there is no EU-wide "High Value Asset" register equivalent to the US OMB HVA program. Instead, NIS2 (Directive (EU) 2022/2555) and the CER Directive ((EU) 2022/2557) classify organisations as "essential"/"important" or "critical entities" and impose risk-based, all-hazards obligations — within which cryptography and PQC migration sit.
- PQC migration is now coordinated EU policy with concrete dates: Commission Recommendation (EU) 2024/1101 (11 April 2024) launched the effort, and the NIS Cooperation Group's "Coordinated Implementation Roadmap" (11 June 2025) sets milestones of end-2026 (national roadmaps + start), **end-2030 (high-risk use cases complete)** and **end-2035 (medium/low-risk complete)**.
- Cryptographic obligations are already legally binding via NIS2 Art. 21(2)(h) (cryptography/encryption), GDPR Art. 32 (encryption, state of the art), DORA, the Cyber Resilience Act, and eIDAS/eIDAS 2.0 trust services — so crypto-agility, cryptographic inventories and PQ/T hybrid schemes are the practical near-term mandate.

## Key Findings

1. **The EU framework is entity-centric and risk-based**, not asset-list-based. This is the single most important structural fact for readers coming from the US HVA model: the EU does not maintain a central "high value asset" list. NIS2 and CER regulate categories of entities; obligations flow from the entity's sector and size, not from a federally designated asset inventory.
2. **Cryptography is already mandatory.** NIS2 Article 21(2)(h) explicitly requires "policies and procedures regarding the use of cryptography and, where appropriate, encryption." GDPR Article 32 names "encryption of personal data" and a "state of the art" test. PQC is the state-of-the-art trajectory; the EU PQC Roadmap explicitly ties to these instruments.
3. **The PQC timeline is now concrete and politically backed**, with end-2030 for high-risk systems and end-2035 for the rest — earlier for high-risk than the US/other 2035 targets.
4. **ENISA is the technical anchor**: its 2021 and 2022 PQC studies, plus its June 2025 NIS2 Technical Implementation Guidance, define the EU's operational expectations, favouring crypto-agility and PQ/T hybrid schemes.
5. **Standards (ISO/IEC 27001, 27005, IEC 62443, ETSI) provide the implementation substrate** that lets entities evidence NIS2 Article 21 compliance and CRA conformity.

## Details

### 1. NIS2 Directive — Directive (EU) 2022/2555
- **Official title:** Directive (EU) 2022/2555 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 14 December 2022 on measures for a high common level of cybersecurity across the Union, amending Regulation (EU) No 910/2014 and Directive (EU) 2018/1972, and repealing Directive (EU) 2016/1148 (NIS 2 Directive).
- **Publisher:** European Parliament and Council. **CELEX:** 32022L2555. **OJ:** L 333, 27.12.2022, p. 80–152.
- **Direct URL:** https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2022/2555/oj/eng
- **Dates:** Entered into force 16 January 2023 (20 days after OJ publication). Transposition deadline **17 October 2024**; rules apply from **18 October 2024**, the date NIS Directive (EU) 2016/1148 was repealed.
- **Essential vs important entities (Article 3):** Essential entities are larger organisations in Annex I (sectors of high criticality) and face proactive supervision (audits, inspections) plus higher fines; important entities (Annex II, plus medium Annex I) face reactive (ex-post) supervision and lower fines. A size-cap rule applies generally (medium-sized = 50+ employees or >€10m turnover/balance sheet); some entities (DNS providers, TLD registries, trust service providers, domain registration services, certain public administration, and entities identified as critical under CER) are in scope regardless of size.
- **Penalties:** Essential entities — up to €10 million or 2% of total worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. Important entities — up to €7 million or 1.4%. Article 20 introduces management-body personal accountability (including possible temporary bans from management functions for essential entities).
- **Annex I (Sectors of high criticality, 11):** Energy (electricity, district heating/cooling, oil, gas, hydrogen); Transport (air, rail, water, road); Banking; Financial market infrastructures; Health; Drinking water; Waste water; Digital infrastructure; ICT service management (B2B); Public administration; Space.
- **Annex II (Other critical sectors, 7):** Postal and courier services; Waste management; Manufacture, production and distribution of chemicals; Production, processing and distribution of food; Manufacturing (medical devices/IVDs; computer/electronic/optical products; electrical equipment; machinery; motor vehicles; other transport equipment); Digital providers (online marketplaces, search engines, social networking platforms); Research.
- **Article 21 risk-management measures** (all-hazards): includes risk-analysis/security policies, incident handling, business continuity/backup/crisis management, **supply chain security (21(2)(d))**, security in acquisition/development/maintenance including vulnerability handling, effectiveness assessment, cyber hygiene/training, **cryptography and encryption policies (21(2)(h))**, HR/access control/asset management, and MFA/secure communications.
- **Article 23 incident reporting:** 24-hour early warning, 72-hour incident notification, and a final report within one month (progress report if ongoing), to the CSIRT/competent authority.
- **Implementing Regulation:** Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2690 of 17 October 2024 sets technical and methodological requirements for Art. 21(2) for digital-infrastructure/ICT/digital-provider entities.
- **Transposition status (for planning):** Following letters of formal notice to 23 Member States on 28 November 2024, the European Commission on 7 May 2025 sent reasoned opinions to **19 Member States** (Bulgaria, Czechia, Denmark, Germany, Estonia, Ireland, Spain, France, Cyprus, Latvia, Luxembourg, Hungary, the Netherlands, Austria, Poland, Portugal, Slovenia, Finland and Sweden) "for failing to notify full transposition of the NIS2 Directive"; only Belgium, Croatia, Italy and Lithuania met the 17 October 2024 deadline. As of mid-2026, the large majority of Member States had adopted transposing legislation while a handful (e.g. France, Ireland, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Spain) were still completing the legislative process — so registration portals, deadlines and fine ceilings differ by Member State. *(Transposition tracking from European Commission infringement communications; national status from secondary compliance trackers — verify per jurisdiction.)*

### 2. CER Directive — Critical Entities Resilience Directive (EU) 2022/2557
- **Official title:** Directive (EU) 2022/2557 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 14 December 2022 on the resilience of critical entities and repealing Council Directive 2008/114/EC.
- **CELEX:** 32022L2557. **OJ:** L 333, 27.12.2022, p. 164–198. **URL:** https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2022/2557/oj/eng
- **Dates:** Transposition deadline **17 October 2024**, apply from 18 October 2024. National resilience strategies by 17 January 2026; Member States must identify/designate critical entities by **17 July 2026**.
- **11 sectors:** energy, transport, banking, financial market infrastructures, health, drinking water, waste water, digital infrastructure, public administration, space, and food.
- **Concept:** A "critical entity" is a public or private entity identified by a Member State as providing essential services and subject to physical-resilience obligations (risk assessment, technical/organisational measures, incident notification). CER addresses **physical/all-hazards resilience**, while cybersecurity is "addressed sufficiently in NIS2" and excluded from CER's scope — the two are deliberately complementary (cyber resilience vs physical resilience).
- **Supplement:** Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/2450 (25 July 2023) establishes a non-exhaustive list of essential services.

### 3. Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) — Regulation (EU) 2024/2847
- **Official title:** Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 23 October 2024 on horizontal cybersecurity requirements for products with digital elements and amending Regulations (EU) No 168/2013 and (EU) 2019/1020 and Directive (EU) 2020/1828 (Cyber Resilience Act).
- **CELEX:** 32024R2847. **OJ:** L, 2024/2847, 20.11.2024. **URL:** https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/2847/oj/eng
- **Official Commission page:** https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cyber-resilience-act ; summary: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cra-summary
- **Dates:** Entered into force **10 December 2024**. Reporting obligations (actively exploited vulnerabilities/severe incidents, via ENISA's single reporting platform) apply from **11 September 2026**; main obligations apply from **11 December 2027**. Conformity-assessment-body notification provisions from 11 June 2026.
- **Scope:** "products with digital elements" (hardware and software with a direct/indirect data connection), including remote data processing solutions; excludes products covered by sector-specific rules (e.g. medical devices, motor vehicles).
- **Essential cybersecurity requirements (Annex I):** secure-by-design/default, vulnerability handling across the support period, free security updates, published vulnerability-disclosure contact, and SBOM obligations. The CRA is the legal anchor for crypto-agility in products: from 11 December 2027 products with lifecycles beyond that date must support secure cryptographic updates.

### 4. DORA — Digital Operational Resilience Act, Regulation (EU) 2022/2554
- **Official title:** Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 14 December 2022 on digital operational resilience for the financial sector and amending Regulations (EC) No 1060/2009, (EU) No 648/2012, (EU) No 600/2014, (EU) No 909/2014 and (EU) 2016/1011.
- **CELEX:** 32022R2554. **OJ:** L 333, 27.12.2022, p. 1–79. **URL:** https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2554/oj/eng
- **Dates:** Entered into force 16 January 2023; **applies from 17 January 2025** (directly applicable, no transposition). The accompanying Directive (EU) 2022/2556 amends sectoral directives.
- **Scope:** 20 types of financial entities as defined in DORA Article 2(1)(a)–(t), plus ICT third-party service providers.
- **Five pillars:** (1) ICT risk management; (2) ICT-related incident reporting; (3) digital operational resilience testing (incl. threat-led penetration testing); (4) ICT third-party risk; (5) information sharing.
- **Oversight framework:** DORA establishes an EU-wide oversight of critical ICT third-party providers (CTPPs), led by the ESAs (EBA, ESMA, EIOPA). On **18 November 2025** the ESAs designated **19 critical ICT third-party providers** under DORA Article 31 — including AWS, Microsoft, Google Cloud, IBM, Oracle, SAP and Deutsche Telekom (list updated annually). Non-compliance can attract periodic penalty payments of up to 1% of average daily worldwide turnover per day of breach (Art. 35(6)).
- **Official ESA page:** https://www.eiopa.europa.eu/digital-operational-resilience-act-dora_en

### 5. GDPR — Regulation (EU) 2016/679
- **Official title:** Regulation (EU) 2016/679 ... (General Data Protection Regulation). **CELEX:** 32016R0679. **OJ:** L 119, 4.5.2016. **URL:** https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng
- **Article 32 (security of processing):** requires technical/organisational measures appropriate to risk, explicitly naming "the pseudonymisation and encryption of personal data," and a "state of the art" test alongside cost, nature, scope and purpose. Recital 83 names encryption as a mitigation. Article 34 covers communication of a personal data breach to data subjects.
- **PQC relevance:** Because Article 32 is technology-neutral and dynamic ("state of the art"), as PQC becomes the recognised standard for protecting long-lived confidential personal data, encryption obligations will increasingly imply quantum-resistant protection — directly relevant to "harvest-now-decrypt-later" risk.

### 6. eIDAS and eIDAS 2.0
- **Original eIDAS — Regulation (EU) No 910/2014** of 23 July 2014 on electronic identification and trust services for electronic transactions in the internal market and repealing Directive 1999/93/EC. **CELEX:** 32014R0910. **OJ:** L 257, 28.8.2014, p. 73–114. Entered into force 17 September 2014; applied from 1 July 2016. **URL:** https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2014/910/oj/eng
- **eIDAS 2.0 — Regulation (EU) 2024/1183** of 11 April 2024 amending Regulation (EU) No 910/2014 as regards establishing the European Digital Identity Framework. **CELEX:** 32024R1183. **OJ:** L, 2024/1183, 30.4.2024. Entered into force **20 May 2024**. **URL:** https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1183/oj/eng
- **EUDI Wallet:** Each Member State must provide at least one EU Digital Identity Wallet to citizens/residents/businesses, with a deadline of **end-2026** (24 months after the relevant implementing acts).
- **PQC relevance:** eIDAS trust services — qualified trust service providers (QTSPs), qualified electronic signatures/seals, and qualified certificates — are PKI-heavy and have among the longest cryptographic transition lead times, making them a priority for PQC migration. eIDAS 2.0 also expands qualified trust services (electronic archiving, electronic ledgers, remote signature/seal device management).

### 7. ENISA Guidance (most important for PQC)
- **"Post-Quantum Cryptography: Current state and quantum mitigation"** (ENISA, v2 May 2021; first version Feb 2021; DOI 10.2824/92307). Landing page: https://www.enisa.europa.eu/publications/post-quantum-cryptography-current-state-and-quantum-mitigation ; PDF: https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/publications/ENISA%20Report%20-%20Post-Quantum%20Cryptography%20Current%20state%20and%20quantum%20mitigation-V2.pdf . Presents the five PQC algorithm families (code-, isogeny-, hash-, lattice-, multivariate-based) and recommends, before standardisation, hybrid implementations (pre-quantum + post-quantum) and mixing pre-shared keys.
- **"Post-Quantum Cryptography — Integration study"** (ENISA, October 2022). Landing page: https://www.enisa.europa.eu/publications/post-quantum-cryptography-integration-study ; PDF: https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/publications/Post%20Quantum%20Cryptography-%20Integration%20Publication.pdf . Addresses integrating PQC into existing protocols; reiterates hybrid combinations to hedge against immaturity of new schemes.
- **"Post-Quantum Cryptography: Anticipating Threats and Preparing the Future"** (ENISA, October 2022) — awareness piece: https://www.enisa.europa.eu/news/enisa-news/post-quantum-cryptography-anticipating-threats-and-preparing-the-future
- **ENISA position on crypto-agility/hybrids:** ENISA recommends a risk-based approach, symmetric mechanisms with adjusted parameter lengths where possible, and PQ/T hybrid schemes to protect against vulnerabilities in newer post-quantum mechanisms.
- **NIS2 Technical Implementation Guidance** (ENISA, v1.0, 26 June 2025, ~170 pages) — companion to Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2690, mirroring its 13-thematic-area Annex structure, with guidance, evidence examples, and mappings to standards (ISO/IEC 27001/27002, NIST CSF 2.0, IEC 62443, ETSI EN 319 401, CEN/TS 18026:2024). Landing page: https://www.enisa.europa.eu/publications/nis2-technical-implementation-guidance ; PDF: https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025-06/ENISA_Technical_implementation_guidance_on_cybersecurity_risk_management_measures_version_1.0.pdf
- **EU coordinated PQC work:**
  - **Commission Recommendation (EU) 2024/1101** of 11 April 2024 on a Coordinated Implementation Roadmap for the transition to Post-Quantum Cryptography. **CELEX:** 32024H1101. **OJ:** L, 2024/1101, 12.4.2024. **URL:** https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reco/2024/1101/oj/eng . Encourages Member States to develop a joint PQC roadmap, deploy PQC via hybrid schemes (possibly combined with QKD), and coordinate via a NIS Cooperation Group sub-group.
  - **"A Coordinated Implementation Roadmap for the Transition to Post-Quantum Cryptography"** (NIS Cooperation Group PQC Workstream, Part 1, v1.1, **11 June 2025**). Commission landing page: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/coordinated-implementation-roadmap-transition-post-quantum-cryptography
    - **By 31.12.2026:** First Steps implemented by all Member States; initial national PQC transition roadmaps established; PQC transition planning and pilots for high- and medium-risk use cases initiated.
    - **By 31.12.2030:** Next Steps implemented; **PQC transition for high-risk use cases completed**; planning/pilots for medium-risk completed; quantum-safe software/firmware upgrades enabled by default. Quantum-vulnerable stand-alone public-key mechanisms not to be used for high-risk use cases after end-2030.
    - **By 31.12.2035:** PQC transition for medium-risk use cases completed; low-risk completed "as much as feasible." (Medium-risk stand-alone quantum-vulnerable public-key mechanisms not to be used after end-2035.)
    - **Risk tiers:** based on the PQC Migration Handbook quantum-risk score (low=1, medium=2, high=3–4); high-risk = long-term confidentiality (10+ years) with significant residual impact, or high transition effort combined with high impact.
    - **Crypto-agility, inventories, hybrids:** cryptographic inventories (preferably CBOM, an SBOM extension) are a "no-regret" first step; crypto-agility is a core objective (and to be systematically implemented from December 2027 per the CRA); standardised, tested **PQ/T hybrid solutions** are recommended "whenever feasible and suitable."
    - **Algorithms:** Part 1 contains no detailed algorithm recommendations and does **not** name ML-KEM/ML-DSA/SLH-DSA; it defers to the ECCG (European Cybersecurity Certification Group) Sub-group on Cryptography "Agreed Cryptographic Mechanisms" v2 (2025), available via ENISA certification: https://certification.enisa.europa.eu/publications/eucc-guidelines-cryptography_en
  - A public consultation on the roadmap ran to 29 September 2025. Separately, the Commission tabled a targeted NIS2 amendment/cybersecurity package in early 2026 (COM(2026) 13) signalling intent to make PQC transition policy an explicit named component of national cybersecurity strategies under NIS2 — **not adopted as of mid-2026** (proposal stage; treat as forward-looking).

### 8. "Critical Assets" / critical digital infrastructure framing — why the EU has no HVA list
- NIS2 and CER frame "critical infrastructure," "digital infrastructure" and "essential services" at the level of **entities and the services they provide**, not designated assets. The CER recital history is explicit that the EU moved "beyond protective measures of individual assets" (the old 2008/114/EC European Critical Infrastructure approach) toward an entity-resilience model.
- Consequently, the EU does **not** maintain a centrally-designated, reported asset inventory analogous to the US OMB HVA program. The obligation is for each entity to perform its own risk assessment and maintain its own asset and cryptographic inventories (reinforced by the PQC Roadmap's "no-regret" cryptographic inventory step).
- "Essential services" are further specified for CER via Delegated Regulation (EU) 2023/2450.

### 9. Standards relationships
- **ISO/IEC 27001:2022** — Information security management systems — Requirements. Official ISO page: https://www.iso.org/standard/27001 . Jointly published by ISO and IEC (JTC 1/SC 27). ENISA's NIS2 mapping treats ISO/IEC 27001:2022 as covering a large share of Article 21 measures (with gaps in incident handling, BC/crisis, supply chain, hygiene/training, and MFA/secure comms requiring explicit NIS2 work).
- **ISO/IEC 27005:2022** — Guidance on managing information security risks. Official ISO page: https://www.iso.org/standard/80585.html (4th edition, published 2022-10; replaces ISO/IEC 27005:2018).
- **IEC 62443 (ISA/IEC 62443)** — security of industrial automation and control systems (IACS/OT). Recognised by IEC as a horizontal standard (2021). Official IEC webstore (series search): https://webstore.iec.ch/en/iec-search/result?q=62443 ; example part IEC 62443-2-1:2024: https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/62883 . Defines security levels (SL 1–4), seven foundational requirements, and secure-development-lifecycle requirements (62443-4-1) relevant to CRA conformity.
- **ETSI TC CYBER QSC (Quantum-Safe Cryptography)** — ETSI working group (ISG QSC founded March 2015; became TC CYBER WG-QSC in March 2017) producing quantum-safe specifications (e.g. TS 103 744 hybrid key establishment). Official ETSI page: https://www.etsi.org/technologies/quantum-safe-cryptography ; TC CYBER: https://www.etsi.org/technical-groups/cyber . ETSI is one of the three European Standards Organisations producing harmonised standards for EU legislation, and its EN 319 4xx trust-service standards underpin eIDAS.
- **How they support compliance:** ISO/IEC 27001/27005 provide the ISMS and risk-management substrate for NIS2 Article 21; IEC 62443 provides the OT/ICS substrate and supports CRA secure-development conformity; ETSI QSC and EN 319 4xx provide the quantum-safe and trust-service technical standards. ENISA's CRA Requirements Standards Mapping (ENISA/JRC, April 2024) maps these standards to CRA essential requirements: https://www.enisa.europa.eu/publications/cyber-resilience-act-requirements-standards-mapping

### 10. Comparative context
- **US OMB High Value Assets:** OMB Memorandum **M-19-03** (10 December 2018) "Strengthening the Cybersecurity of Federal Agencies by Enhancing the High Value Asset Program" defines three HVA categories: **Informational Value, Mission Essential, and Federal Civilian Enterprise Essential (FCEE)**. HVAs are a centrally-designated, reported, assessed, and remediated asset model (agencies report to CISA/DHS). Source: https://www.cio.gov/policies-and-priorities/management-HVA (and the memorandum PDF at whitehouse.gov). This is the structural opposite of the EU's entity-based model. *(Non-EU source — US Government.)*
- **UK approach:** The NCSC **Cyber Assessment Framework (CAF)** is an outcomes-based framework (4 objectives, 14 principles, ~39 contributing outcomes) organised around protecting **"essential functions,"** used for the UK NIS Regulations and GovAssure (current version CAF v4.0, August 2025). The UK uses "essential functions"/"critical assets" ("crown jewels") at the organisational level and, like the EU, has **no central HVA list**. Source: https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/cyber-assessment-framework *(Non-EU source — UK NCSC.)*

## Recommendations
1. **Start with a cryptographic inventory now (no-regret).** Per the EU PQC Roadmap, build and maintain an inventory of all assets performing or subject to cryptographic operations, ideally in a CBOM (SBOM-extension) format. This is the foundation for every later step and is achievable before any algorithm mandate. **Benchmark:** a complete, continuously updated crypto inventory and dependency map by end-2026.
2. **Map your regulatory exposure.** Determine whether you are an essential/important entity under NIS2, a critical entity under CER, a financial entity/CTPP under DORA, a product manufacturer under the CRA, and/or a trust service provider under eIDAS. Each adds distinct cryptographic and reporting obligations; DORA is lex specialis for finance.
3. **Treat crypto-agility as the near-term deliverable.** Because the CRA requires secure update mechanisms from 11 December 2027 and the Roadmap calls for systematic crypto-agility, design products and systems for modular cryptographic replacement and PQ/T hybrid support now.
4. **Prioritise by quantum-risk tier.** Classify use cases high/medium/low using the Roadmap's methodology. Target high-risk use cases (long-lived confidentiality, identity/PKI, software/firmware signing) for completion by end-2030; medium/low by end-2035. eIDAS trust services and long-retention personal data (GDPR) should be high-priority.
5. **Adopt hybrid, standardised schemes.** Follow ENISA and the Roadmap: deploy standardised, tested PQ/T hybrids whenever feasible; consider symmetric alternatives where appropriate. Track the ECCG "Agreed Cryptographic Mechanisms" for the EU's algorithm position rather than assuming NIST FIPS algorithms (ML-KEM/ML-DSA/SLH-DSA) are formally EU-endorsed by name.
6. **Use existing standards to evidence compliance.** Anchor NIS2 Article 21 to ISO/IEC 27001/27005 and (for OT) IEC 62443; use ENISA's NIS2 Technical Implementation Guidance (v1.0, June 2025) for evidence examples; use ENISA/JRC CRA Standards Mapping for CRA conformity.
- **Thresholds that change the plan:** a credible cryptographically relevant quantum computer ("Q-Day") arriving before 2030; adoption of COM(2026) 13 making PQC an explicit NIS2 national-strategy requirement; publication of EU-endorsed PQC algorithm names by the ECCG; and national transposition deltas (several Member States transposed NIS2/CER late).

## Caveats
- **Some dates are recommendation/policy rather than hard statutory deadlines.** The PQC Roadmap milestones (2026/2030/2035) are recommendations by the NIS Cooperation Group, not directly enforceable regulation; however, they are increasingly anchored to binding instruments (CRA dates, NIS2 national strategies).
- **The NIS2 Annex sector wording** should be verified verbatim against the EUR-Lex text for legal use; the full ELI/HTML page exceeds automated fetch limits, so sub-clause detail (NACE divisions, cross-references) should be checked directly at https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2022/2555/oj/eng. Sector counts (11 + 7 = 18) and names are uniformly corroborated across the EUR-Lex summary and the directive text.
- **National transposition varies.** As of 2025–2026 several Member States had not fully transposed NIS2/CER; entity-level obligations, registration portals and fine ceilings differ by Member State and must be checked per jurisdiction.
- **ISO and IEC standards are paywalled**; only their official catalogue pages (cited) are freely linkable. ETSI deliverables are free.
- **Non-EU sources** (US OMB M-19-03; UK NCSC CAF) are clearly marked and used only for comparison. A few operational data points (e.g. DORA CTPP names, NIS2 national transposition status) draw on reputable secondary trackers and EU press communications rather than a single consolidated EUR-Lex text — verify the live list/status before publication.
- **Forward-looking items** (COM(2026) 13 PQC-in-NIS2 proposal; Roadmap Part 2 with technical detail; future ECCG algorithm lists) are proposals/expected deliverables, not yet adopted law, and are flagged as such.
